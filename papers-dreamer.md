## [2018] [hardmaru] [World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122)

- **Date**: 2026-01-21
- **Blog**: <https://worldmodels.github.io/>

---

- **Abstract**:
  - > We explore building generative neural network models of popular reinforcement learning environments. Our world model can be trained quickly in an unsupervised manner to learn a compressed spatial and temporal representation of the environment. By using features extracted from the world model as inputs to an agent, we can train a very compact and simple policy that can solve the required task. We can even train our agent entirely inside of its own dream environment generated by its world model, and transfer this policy back into the actual environment.
- **Core idea**:
  - Learn a compact, action-conditioned predictive model of the environment first, then learn the policy through that model rather than directly from pixels.
  - The learned model is not merely an auxiliary prediction task. It can become the synthetic environment in which behavior is trained.
  - The architecture is divided into **V**, **M**, and **C**: a visual encoder, a recurrent dynamics model, and a small controller.
- **Architecture — V, M, C**:
  - **Vision model $V$ — compress the observation**:
    - A VAE maps an image $x_t$ to a small stochastic latent vector:
      $$
      z_t \sim q_\phi(z_t \mid x_t).
      $$
    - It is trained with reconstruction loss plus a KL penalty toward a standard Gaussian. Its purpose is to turn a high-dimensional image into a state that is easier to predict and control from.
    - Because the VAE is trained frame by frame and without the control objective, the representation is task-agnostic: it can preserve visually prominent details while discarding small but control-relevant features.
  - **Memory model $M$ — predict latent dynamics**:
    - An LSTM receives the current latent state and action and updates its deterministic memory:
      $$
      h_{t+1} = f_\theta(h_t, z_t, a_t).
      $$
    - An MDN head predicts a distribution over the next latent state:
      $$
      p_\theta(z_{t+1} \mid h_{t+1}).
      $$
    - The mixture distribution allows multiple possible futures instead of forcing a deterministic predictor to average them together.
    - $z_t$ represents compressed information from the current observation; $h_t$ carries history and predictive context over time. This is the conceptual ancestor of the deterministic-plus-stochastic recurrent state used by PlaNet and Dreamer.
    - For the Doom experiment, $M$ also predicts episode termination. It is not yet the fully integrated modern world model with general observation, reward, and discount heads.
  - **Controller $C$ — act from the model state**:
    - The controller receives both the current latent and the recurrent memory:
      $$
      a_t = C_\psi(z_t, h_t).
      $$
    - Because $V$ and $M$ do most of the representational work, $C$ can be extremely small—approximately a linear policy.
    - The paper optimizes $C$ with CMA-ES, a black-box evolutionary method, rather than differentiating the control objective through $M$.
- **Staged training procedure**:
  - (1) Collect trajectories, largely using a random policy.
  - (2) Train $V$ to reconstruct individual frames.
  - (3) Encode the trajectories into sequences of $z_t$.
  - (4) Train $M$ by maximum likelihood to predict the next latent distribution.
  - (5) Freeze $V$ and $M$, then optimize $C$.
  - This modularity makes the idea simple to demonstrate, but the representation is not trained for control, the components are not jointly optimized, policy learning cannot improve the model, and random data may not cover the states later visited by the learned policy.
- **Intro**:
  - > To handle the vast amount of information that flows through our daily lives, our brain learns an abstract representation of both spatial and temporal aspects of this information. We are able to observe a scene and remember an abstract description thereof (Cheang & Tsao, 2017; Quiroga et al., 2005). Evidence also suggests that what we perceive at any given moment is governed by our brain’s prediction of the future based on our internal model (Nortmann et al., 2015; Gerrit et al., 2013).
  - > One way of understanding the predictive model inside of our brains is that it might not be about just predicting the future in  general,  but  predicting  future  sensory  data  given  our current motor actions (Keller et al., 2012; Leinweber et al., 2017).  We are able to instinctively act on this predictive model and perform fast reflexive behaviours when we face danger (Mobbs et al., 2015), without the need to consciously plan out a course of action.
  - > A batter has milliseconds to decide how they should swing the bat – shorter than the time it takes for visual signals to reach our brain.  The reason we are able to hit a 100 mph fastball is due to our ability to instinctively predict when and where the ball will go. For professional players, this all happens subconsciously. Their muscles reflexively swing the bat at the right time and loca- tion in line with their internal models’ predictions (Gerrit et al., 2013).  They can quickly act on their predictions of the future without the need to consciously roll out possible future scenarios to form a plan (Hirshon, 2013).
  - > Ideally, we would like to be able to efficiently train large RNN-based agents. The backpropagation algorithm [23, 24, 25] can be used to train large neural networks efficiently. In this work we look at training a large neural network2 to tackle RL tasks, by dividing the agent into a large world model and a small controller model. We first train a large neural network to learn a model of the agent’s world in an unsupervised manner, and then train the smaller controller model to learn to perform a task using this world model. A small controller lets the training algorithm focus on the credit assignment problem on a small search space, while not sacrificing capacity and expressiveness via the larger world model. By training the agent through the lens of its world model, we show that it can learn a highly compact policy to perform its task.
  - > Most existing model-based RL[29, 30] approaches learn a model of the RL environment, but still train on the actual environment. Here, we also explore fully replacing an actual RL environment with a generated one, training our agent’s controller only inside of the environment generated by its own internal world model, and transfer this policy back into the actual environment.
- **World Model for Feature Extraction**:
  - > Since our world model is able to model the future, we are also able to have it come up with hypothetical car racing scenarios on its own.
  - > We have just seen that a policy learned inside of the real environment appears to somewhat function inside of the dream environment. This begs the question — can we train our agent to learn inside of its own dream, and transfer this policy back to the actual environment?
- **Learning Inside of a Dream**:
  - > If our world model is sufficiently accurate for its purpose, and complete enough for the problem at hand, we should be able to substitute the actual environment with this world model. After all, our agent does not directly observe the reality, but only sees what the world model lets it see.
  - > Unlike the actual game environment, however, we note that it is possible to add extra uncertainty into the virtual environment, thus making the game more challenging in the dream environment. We can do this by increasing the temperature $\tau$ parameter during the sampling process of $z_{t+1}$. By increasing the uncertainty, our dream environment becomes more difficult compared to the actual environment. The fireballs may move more randomly in a less predictable path compared to the actual game. Sometimes the agent may even die due to sheer misfortune, without explanation
  - Once $M$ has been learned, it can replace the real environment during controller training:
    $$
    (z_t, h_t) \xrightarrow{C} a_t \xrightarrow{M} (z_{t+1}, h_{t+1}).
    $$
  - The controller can learn from these imagined latent trajectories without running the real environment or generating pixels. Decoding $z_t$ back into an image is useful for human inspection, but unnecessary for the agent.
  - This is the main idea inherited by Dreamer: learn behavior from consequences generated inside a compact latent dynamics model.
- **Cheating the World Model**:
  - > As a result of using M to generate a virtual environment for our agent, we are also giving the controller access to all of the hidden states of M. This is essentially granting our agent access to all of the internal states and memory of the game engine, rather than only the game observations that the player gets to see. Therefore our agent can efficiently explore ways to directly manipulate the hidden states of the game engine in its quest to maximize its expected cumulative reward. The weakness of this approach of learning a policy inside of a learned dynamics model is that our agent can easily find an adversarial policy that can fool our dynamics model — it will find a policy that looks good under our dynamics model, but will fail in the actual environment, usually because it visits states where the model is wrong because they are away from the training distribution.
  - Policy optimization actively searches for weaknesses in the learned model. It can find trajectories that appear high-return under $M$ but fail in reality because they leave the distribution on which $M$ was trained.
  - Increasing the MDN-RNN sampling temperature $\tau$ makes the dream more stochastic and difficult, reducing the controller's ability to depend on one brittle predicted future.
  - The lasting lesson is broader than this temperature heuristic:
    $$
    \text{policy optimization pressure}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \text{exploitation of model error}.
    $$
  - Controlling model bias and keeping imagined states near well-modeled regions remain central problems in model-based RL.
- **Iterative Training Procedure**:
  - > In our experiments, the tasks are relatively simple, so a reasonable world model can be trained using a dataset collected from a random policy. But what if our environments become more sophisticated? In any difficult environment, only parts of the world are made available to the agent only after it learns how to strategically navigate through its world.
  - > For more complicated tasks, an iterative training procedure is required. We need our agent to be able to explore its world, and constantly collect new observations so that its world model can be improved and refined over time.
  - > An interesting connection to the neuroscience literature is the work on hippocampal replay that examines how the brain replays recent experiences when an animal rests or sleeps. Replaying recent experiences plays an important role in memory consolidation [68] — where hippocampus-dependent memories become independent of the hippocampus over a period of time [67]. As Foster [68] puts it, replay is “less like dreaming and more like thought”. We invite readers to read Replay Comes of Age [68] for a detailed overview of replay from a neuroscience perspective with connections to theoretical reinforcement learning.
- **Discussion**:
  - > We have demonstrated the possibility of training an agent to perform tasks entirely inside of its simulated latent space world. This approach offers many practical benefits. For instance, video game engines typically require heavy compute resources for rendering the game states into image frames, or calculating physics not immediately relevant to the game. We may not want to waste cycles training an agent in the actual environment, but instead train the agent as many times as we want inside its simulated environment. Agents that are trained incrementally to simulate reality may prove to be useful for transferring policies back to the real world. Our approach may complement sim2real approaches outlined in previous work.
- > The benefit of implementing the world model as a fully differentiable recurrent computation graph also means that we may be able to train our agents in the dream directly using the backpropagation algorithm to fine-tune its policy to maximize an objective function.
- > The choice of implementing V as a VAE and training it as a standalone model also has its limitations, since it may encode parts of the observations that are not relevant to a task. After all, unsupervised learning cannot, by definition, know what will be useful for the task at hand. For instance, our VAE reproduced unimportant detailed brick tile patterns on the side walls in the Doom environment, but failed to reproduce task-relevant tiles on the road in the Car Racing environment. By training together with an M that predicts rewards, the VAE may learn to focus on task-relevant areas of the image, but the tradeoff here is that we may not be able to reuse the VAE effectively for new tasks without retraining. **Learning task-relevant features has connections to neuroscience as well. Primary sensory neurons are released from inhibition when rewards are received, which suggests that they generally learn task-relevant features, rather than just any features, at least in adulthood**.
- > Another concern is the limited capacity of our world model. While modern storage devices can store large amounts of historical data generated using an iterative training procedure, our LSTM-based [115, 116] world model may not be able to store all of the recorded information inside of its weight connections. While the human brain can hold decades and even centuries of memories to some resolution [117], our neural networks trained with backpropagation have more limited capacity and suffer from issues such as catastrophic forgetting [118, 119, 120]. Future work will explore replacing the VAE and MDN-RNN with higher capacity models [121, 122, 123, 124, 125], or incorporating an external memory module [126], if we want our agent to learn to explore more complicated worlds.
- **Bridge to PlaNet and Dreamer**:
  - **Separate framewise VAE and dynamics $\rightarrow$ jointly trained RSSM**: PlaNet combines representation and dynamics learning in one recurrent state-space model.
  - **Loosely coupled $z_t$ and $h_t$ $\rightarrow$ explicit stochastic and deterministic state**: the RSSM formalizes $z_t$ as the stochastic state and $h_t$ as deterministic recurrent memory.
  - **No clean observed/imagination distinction $\rightarrow$ posterior and prior**:
    $$
    \underbrace{q(z_t \mid h_t, x_t)}_{\text{posterior: infer from a real observation}}
    \qquad\text{versus}\qquad
    \underbrace{p(z_t \mid h_t)}_{\text{prior: predict without the observation}}.
    $$
    - When learning from replayed experience, the model can use the posterior because $x_t$ is available. During imagination, future observations do not exist, so it must roll forward using the prior.
    - The KL term trains the prior to match the posterior closely enough that imagined rollouts remain useful.
  - **CMA-ES controller $\rightarrow$ differentiable behavior learning**: Dreamer trains an actor and critic on imagined trajectories and propagates analytic gradients through the learned dynamics.
  - **No learned long-term value $\rightarrow$ value bootstrapping**: finite imagination horizons are extended with a learned critic, reducing the need for very long and increasingly inaccurate model rollouts.
  - **Fixed random dataset $\rightarrow$ continual learning loop**: Dreamer repeatedly alternates environment interaction, replay-based world-model learning, and behavior learning in imagination.
  - **Lineage after the original Dreamer**: DreamerV2 introduces discrete stochastic latents for Atari; DreamerV3 emphasizes robustness across many domains with one configuration; Dreamer 4 scales the world model with a transformer and learns heavily from offline video.
- **What to retain**:
  - Compress pixels into stochastic latent states.
  - Use recurrent memory to make those states predictive over time.
  - Treat learned latent dynamics as a synthetic environment.
  - Learn behavior from imagined consequences rather than only real interaction.
  - Expect the policy to exploit every error in the learned model.
  - Dreamer's fundamental contribution is not inventing the dream; World Models already supplies that idea. Dreamer turns it into an integrated, differentiable actor–critic procedure.

## [2018] [Dreamer0] [PlaNet: Learning Latent Dynamics for Planning from Pixels](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04551)

- **Date**: 2026-03-18
- **Blog**: <https://research.google/blog/introducing-planet-a-deep-planning-network-for-reinforcement-learning/>
- **Code**: <https://github.com/google-research/planet>

---

- **Abstract**:
  - > **Planning  has  been  very  successful  for  control tasks  with  known  environment  dynamics.   To leverage  planning  in  unknown  environments, the  agent  needs  to  learn  the  dynamics  from interactions with the world.**  However, learning dynamics models that are accurate enough for planning  has  been  a  long-standing  challenge, especially in image-based domains. We propose the Deep Planning Network (PlaNet),  **a purely model-based agent that learns the environment dynamics  from  images  and  chooses  actions through fast online planning in latent space.  To achieve high performance, the dynamics model must  accurately  predict  the  rewards  ahead  for multiple time steps**.  We approach this using a **latent dynamics model with both deterministic and stochastic transition components**. Moreover, we  propose  a  multi-step  variational  inference objective   that   we   name   latent   overshooting. Using only pixel observations, our agent solves continuous control tasks with contact dynamics, partial observability, and sparse rewards, which exceed the difficulty of tasks that were previously solved by planning with learned models. PlaNet uses substantially fewer episodes and reaches final performance close to and sometimes higher than strong model-free algorithms.
- **Intro**:
  - > Planning is a natural and powerful approach to decision making problems with known dynamics.
  - > To leverage planning in unknown environments, the agent needs to learn the dynamics from interactions with the world.
  - Prior work on planning assumed access to the underlying state and reward function — not available in practice. Successful latent models were limited to simple tasks like balancing cartpoles from dense rewards.
  - Learning dynamics that are accurate enough for planning is hard due to model inaccuracies, accumulating errors of multi-step predictions, failure to capture multiple possible futures, and overconfident predictions outside of the training distribution.
  - **Planning has two advantages over model-free RL**:
    - (1) **sample efficiency**: leverage a richer training signal without propagating rewards through Bellman backups,
    - (2) **test-time compute scaling**: performance can improve just by increasing compute budget for action search.
  - **Three contributions**:
    - (1) **Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM)**: latent dynamics with both deterministic and stochastic components, both shown to be crucial,
    - (2) **Latent Overshooting**: multi-step variational objective trained purely in latent space,
    - (3) solves visual continuous control with 200× fewer episodes than model-free baselines.
- **Notation used below**:
  - The paper writes image observations as $o_t$, stochastic latent states as $s_t$, and deterministic recurrent states as $h_t$. To avoid overloading $s_t$ with both observations and model states across these notes, use $x_t$ for images and $z_t$ for stochastic latents below.
- **Section 2 — Latent dynamics and online planning**:
  - PlaNet learns a transition model, observation model, and reward model from previously experienced episodes, together with a filtering encoder that infers the current latent state from the observation history.
  - The observation decoder supplies a rich learning signal but is **not used for planning**. Candidate futures are evaluated by rolling forward the latent transition and reward models only.
  - PlaNet implements its policy through **model predictive control (MPC)** rather than a policy or value network:
    $$
    a_{t:t+H-1}^{*}
    =
    \arg\max_{a_{t:t+H-1}}
    \mathbb{E}_{p_\theta}
    \left[
    \sum_{\tau=t}^{t+H-1} r_\tau
    \right].
    $$
    - Execute only the first action $a_t^*$, incorporate the next real observation into the latent-state posterior, and solve a new planning problem. Replanning every step lets the agent correct its plan using new evidence.
  - **Cross-entropy method (CEM)** approximates the action-sequence search:
    - Initialize a time-dependent diagonal Gaussian over action sequences with zero mean and unit variance.
    - Sample $J$ candidate sequences, roll one stochastic latent trajectory for each, and sum the predicted mean rewards.
    - Keep the top $K$ elite sequences and refit the Gaussian; repeat for $I$ iterations.
    - Execute the mean action for the current step. PlaNet resets the action-sequence distribution before replanning at the next environment step rather than warm-starting it.
  - **Experience collection** is iterative: start with a small set of random seed episodes, repeatedly train the model, then collect another episode by planning with the partially trained model while adding Gaussian exploration noise.
- **Section 3 — Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM)**:
  - A purely deterministic RNN can remember history but cannot represent multiple possible futures and can be easy for the planner to exploit.
  - A purely stochastic state-space model can represent uncertainty, but forcing information through a sampled bottleneck at every step makes long-term memory difficult.
  - RSSM combines a deterministic recurrent path with a stochastic state:
    - Deterministic state:
      $$
      h_t = f_\theta(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1}).
      $$
    - Predictive prior, used without the current observation:
      $$
      z_t \sim p_\theta(z_t \mid h_t).
      $$
    - Filtering posterior, used when the real observation is available:
      $$
      z_t \sim q_\phi(z_t \mid h_t, x_t).
      $$
    - Observation and reward models:
      $$
      x_t \sim p_\theta(x_t \mid h_t,z_t),
      \qquad
      r_t \sim p_\theta(r_t \mid h_t,z_t).
      $$
  - The compact model state is the pair $(h_t,z_t)$: $h_t$ provides stable memory; $z_t$ represents the stochastic information needed to explain the current observation and possible futures.
  - All information from an observation must pass through the sampled $z_t$, preventing a deterministic shortcut from the encoder to the reconstruction that the transition prior could not reproduce.
  - **Real experience follows the posterior; planning follows the prior**:
    - On replayed data, $q_\phi(z_t \mid h_t,x_t)$ corrects the state estimate using the observed frame.
    - In a candidate future, $x_t$ does not exist, so the planner must sample from $p_\theta(z_t \mid h_t)$.
  - **Training objective** — the paper writes the observation-only variational bound and notes that reward prediction follows analogously. Including both signals, the objective has the form:
    $$
    \mathcal{J}
    =
    \sum_t
    \mathbb{E}_q
    \left[
      \ln p_\theta(x_t \mid h_t,z_t)
      + \ln p_\theta(r_t \mid h_t,z_t)
    \right]
    -
    \sum_t
    \mathbb{E}_q
    \left[
      \operatorname{KL}
      \left(
        q_\phi(z_t \mid h_t,x_t)
        \,\|\,
        p_\theta(z_t \mid h_t)
      \right)
    \right].
    $$
    - Reconstruction and reward prediction make the state informative; the KL trains the observation-free prior toward the observation-informed posterior so that imagined states resemble states inferred from experience.
- **Section 4 — Latent overshooting**:
  - The standard ELBO directly trains only one-step stochastic predictions. Gradients reach each one-step prior but do not traverse a chain of several stochastic transitions, even though planning requires accurate multi-step rollouts.
  - Let $p_\theta^{(d)}$ denote the distribution obtained by starting from a posterior state, applying the action-conditioned transition $d$ times, and marginalizing the intermediate stochastic states:
    $$
    p_\theta^{(d)}
    \left(
      z_t
      \mid
      h_{t-d},z_{t-d},a_{t-d:t-1}
    \right).
    $$
  - Latent overshooting adds consistency losses for distances $1 \leq d \leq D$:
    $$
    \operatorname{KL}
    \left[
      q_\phi(z_t \mid h_t,x_t)
      \,\|\,
      p_\theta^{(d)}(z_t \mid h_{t-d},z_{t-d},a_{t-d:t-1})
    \right].
    $$
    - This trains multi-step priors toward the corresponding observation-informed posterior entirely in latent space, avoiding the expense of decoding and reconstructing an image at every prediction distance.
    - For overshooting distances $d>1$, gradients are stopped through the posterior target so that the multi-step prediction moves toward the informed posterior rather than the posterior moving toward an inaccurate prediction.
    - The $\beta_d$ weights control the strength assigned to each prediction distance.
  - **Important nuance**: several tested dynamics models benefit from latent overshooting, but the paper explicitly reports that its final RSSM-based PlaNet agent does not require it. RSSM itself, joint latent learning, and MPC are the central pieces to carry into Dreamer.
- **PlaNet in one sentence**: infer the current RSSM state with the posterior, search over action sequences by rolling the prior and reward model forward with CEM, execute one action, observe, and replan.

## [2019] [Dreamer1] [Dream to Control: Learning Behaviors by Latent Imagination](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01603)

- **Date**: 2026-03-18
- **Code**: <https://github.com/google-research/dreamer>

---

- **Abstract**:
  - > Learned  world  models  summarize  an  agent’s  experience  to  facilitate  learning complex behaviors. While learning world models from high-dimensional sensory inputs is becoming feasible through deep learning, there are many potential ways for deriving behaviors from them. We present Dreamer, a reinforcement learning agent that solves long-horizon tasks from images purely by latent imagination. We efficiently learn behaviors by propagating analytic gradients of learned state values back through trajectories imagined in the compact state space of a learned world model. On 20 challenging visual control tasks, Dreamer exceeds existing approaches in data-efficiency, computation time, and final performance.
- **Intro**:
  - > **Intelligent agents can achieve goals in complex environments even though they never encounter the exact same situation twice. This ability requires building  representations  of  the  world  from  past  experience  that  enable generalization to novel situations**.
  - > World models offer an explicit way to represent an agent’s knowledge about the world in a parametric model that can make predictions about the future.
  - [Fig1] **Dreamer**:
    - an agent that learns long-horizon behaviors from images purely by latent imagination.
    - > A novel actor critic algorithm accounts for rewards beyond the imagination horizon while making efficient use of the neural network dynamics.
    - > The values optimize Bellman consistency for imagined rewards and the policy maximizes the values by propagating their analytic gradients back through the dynamics.
    - > **In comparison to actor critic algorithms that learn online or by experience replay, world models can interpolate past experience and offer analytic gradients of multi-step returns for efficient policy optimization**.
    - **With a world model, the environment dynamics becomes differentiable**.
- **Core change from PlaNet**:
  - Dreamer retains PlaNet's learned RSSM but replaces CEM planning with an action model and value model trained on trajectories imagined by the RSSM.
  - PlaNet spends substantial computation searching when each action is needed. Dreamer amortizes that search into an actor: imagination happens during behavior learning, and acting in the real environment requires only a policy forward pass.
- [Fig3, Algorithm1] **Section 2 — Control with world models**:
  - **Compact state notation**:
    - The Dreamer paper denotes the complete Markovian model state by $s_t$. In its RSSM implementation, this state contains both deterministic memory and a stochastic latent—conceptually $s_t=(h_t,z_t)$.
    - The action and value models consume this **complete RSSM state**, not only the stochastic component $z_t$.
  - **Paper-specific $p/q$ convention**: Dreamer uses $p$ for distributions that produce samples associated with real experience and $q$ for learned approximations used in imagination. This is opposite the more common convention used in the PlaNet notes above.
  - The latent dynamics contain three components:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{Representation:}\quad
      &p_\theta(s_t \mid s_{t-1},a_{t-1},x_t),\\
    \text{Transition:}\quad
      &q_\theta(s_t \mid s_{t-1},a_{t-1}),\\
    \text{Reward:}\quad
      &q_\theta(r_t \mid s_t).
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    - The representation model incorporates the real observation and corresponds to posterior state inference.
    - The transition model predicts the next model state without seeing the corresponding future observation and therefore supplies the imagination dynamics.
    - > **the transition model lets us predict ahead in the compact latent space without having to observe or imagine the corresponding images**. This results in a low memory footprint and fast predictions of thousands of imagined trajectories in parallel.
    - > The model mimics a non-linear Kalman filter (Kalman, 1960), latent state space model, or HMM with real-valued states. However, it is conditioned on actions and predicts rewards, allowing the agent to imagine the outcomes of potential action sequences without executing them in the environment.
  - Dreamer alternates three operations throughout its lifetime:
    - (1) **Dynamics learning**: infer RSSM states from replayed experience and update the world model.
    - (2) **Behavior learning**: start imagined trajectories from those inferred states, then train action and value models using predicted transitions and rewards.
    - (3) **Environment interaction**: execute the action model with exploration noise and add the resulting episode to replay.
- **Section 3 — Learning behaviors by latent imagination**:
  - **Imagination environment**:
    - The RSSM model state is treated as a fully observed MDP because $s_t$ is constructed to be Markovian.
    - Imagined trajectories branch from posterior states inferred from replay sequences, then evolve without observations:
      $$
      a_\tau \sim q_\phi(a_\tau \mid s_\tau),
      \qquad
      s_{\tau+1} \sim q_\theta(s_{\tau+1} \mid s_\tau,a_\tau),
      \qquad
      r_\tau \sim q_\theta(r_\tau \mid s_\tau).
      $$
    - The image decoder is unnecessary during imagination. The transition, reward, action, and value networks are sufficient.
    - The world-model parameters $\theta$ are held fixed during behavior learning; gradients update the action and value models rather than changing the imagined environment to make the policy look better.
  - **Action and value models**:
    - The action model is a tanh-transformed Gaussian:
      $$
      a_\tau
      =
      \tanh\!\left(
        \mu_\phi(s_\tau)
        + \sigma_\phi(s_\tau)\epsilon
      \right),
      \qquad
      \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I).
      $$
      - Reparameterization makes the sampled action differentiable with respect to the actor parameters.
    - The value model estimates the long-run return achieved by the current action model from a model state:
      $$
      v_\psi(s_\tau)
      \approx
      \mathbb{E}
      \left[
        \sum_{n=\tau}^{\infty}
        \gamma^{n-\tau}r_n
        \;\middle|\;s_\tau
      \right].
      $$
  - **Value estimation** — imagined rollouts have a finite horizon $H$, so Dreamer combines observed imagined rewards with value bootstrapping:
    - Reward-only horizon return:
      $$
      V_R(s_\tau)
      \triangleq
      \mathbb{E}_{q_\theta,q_\phi}
      \left[
        \sum_{n=\tau}^{t+H} r_n
      \right].
      $$
      - It ignores rewards beyond the imagination horizon and corresponds to the paper's no-value-model ablation.
    - $k$-step bootstrapped return:
      $$
      V_N^k(s_\tau)
      \triangleq
      \mathbb{E}_{q_\theta,q_\phi}
      \left[
        \sum_{n=\tau}^{h-1}\gamma^{n-\tau}r_n
        + \gamma^{h-\tau}v_\psi(s_h)
      \right],
      \qquad
      h=\min(\tau+k,t+H).
      $$
    - $\lambda$-return:
      $$
      V_\lambda(s_\tau)
      \triangleq
      (1-\lambda)
      \sum_{k=1}^{H-1}
      \lambda^{k-1}V_N^k(s_\tau)
      +
      \lambda^{H-1}V_N^H(s_\tau).
      $$
      - This exponentially weighted mixture balances the bias of short bootstrapped returns against the model error and variance of longer returns.
  - **Learning objectives**:
    - Actor:
      $$
      \max_\phi
      \mathbb{E}_{q_\theta,q_\phi}
      \left[
        \sum_{\tau=t}^{t+H}V_\lambda(s_\tau)
      \right].
      $$
    - Critic:
      $$
      \min_\psi
      \mathbb{E}_{q_\theta,q_\phi}
      \left[
        \sum_{\tau=t}^{t+H}
        \frac{1}{2}
        \left\|v_\psi(s_\tau)-\operatorname{sg}\!\left(V_\lambda(s_\tau)\right)\right\|^2
      \right].
      $$
    - The actor's gradient follows the differentiable path
      $$
      \phi
      \longrightarrow a_\tau
      \longrightarrow s_{\tau+1}
      \longrightarrow \{r_{\tau+1},v_\psi(s_{\tau+1})\}
      \longrightarrow V_\lambda.
      $$
      This is the central Dreamer contribution: analytic multi-step value gradients propagate through actions and learned transitions, rather than using CEM or only a score-function policy gradient.
    - Continuous actions and latent states use reparameterized gradients; the paper notes straight-through gradients for discrete actions.
    - For tasks with early termination, the world model predicts a discount from each latent state, and objective terms are weighted by the cumulative product of predicted discounts.
  - **Relation to conventional actor–critic methods**:
    - REINFORCE-family methods such as A3C and PPO use the critic mainly as a variance-reducing baseline; Dreamer differentiates through predicted values, rewards, and dynamics.
    - DDPG and SAC differentiate the critic $Q(s_t,a_t)$ with respect to the current action, but do not propagate the actor gradient through a learned sequence of transitions.
    - Dreamer can use a state value $V(s)$ instead of $Q(s,a)$ because the gradient reaches the action through the differentiable dynamics.
- **Section 4 — Learning latent dynamics**:
  - Dreamer's behavior-learning method is compatible with different world-model objectives. The paper compares reward-only learning, image reconstruction, and contrastive estimation while holding behavior learning fixed.
  - **Reward prediction only**:
    - In principle, a representation that predicts all future rewards from actions and history is sufficient for a fixed task.
    - In finite datasets—especially with sparse rewards—the reward signal is too weak to reliably preserve all observation features that later matter for control.
  - **Image reconstruction** — the default and best-performing objective in most tasks:
    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{J}_O^t
      &\triangleq \ln q_\theta(x_t \mid s_t),\\
    \mathcal{J}_R^t
      &\triangleq \ln q_\theta(r_t \mid s_t),\\
    \mathcal{J}_D^t
      &\triangleq
      -\beta\,
      \operatorname{KL}
      \left[
        p_\theta(s_t \mid s_{t-1},a_{t-1},x_t)
        \,\|\,
        q_\theta(s_t \mid s_{t-1},a_{t-1})
      \right],\\
    \mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{REC}}
      &\triangleq
      \mathbb{E}
      \left[
        \sum_t
        \left(
          \mathcal{J}_O^t
          +\mathcal{J}_R^t
          +\mathcal{J}_D^t
        \right)
      \right]
      +\mathrm{const}.
    \end{aligned}
    $$
    - The observation decoder is a transposed CNN, the reward model is a dense network, and all world-model components are optimized jointly.
  - **Contrastive estimation**:
    - Replaces the observation decoder with a state model $q_\theta(s_t\mid x_t)$ and an NCE-style term:
      $$
      \mathcal{J}_S^t
      \triangleq
      \ln q_\theta(s_t\mid x_t)
      -
      \ln\sum_{x'}q_\theta(s_t\mid x').
      $$
    - The positive term makes the model state predictable from its matching image; the batch of alternative observations supplies negatives that prevent collapse.
    - It avoids pixel generation but solves only about half the tasks and underperforms reconstruction overall.
  - The paper also reports that latent overshooting did not improve Dreamer's model learning in these experiments.
- **Dreamer 1 in one sentence**: infer RSSM states from replay, imagine short trajectories with the observation-free transition model, train a critic with $\lambda$-returns, and train the actor by differentiating those returns through the entire imagined trajectory.
